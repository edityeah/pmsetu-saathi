# PM-SETU scheme — what it is

## Basics
PM-SETU (Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs) is a central sector scheme of the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE), run through the Directorate General of Training (DGT). Cabinet approval 7 May 2025; launched 4 October 2025. Outlay ₹60,000 crore over five years: Centre ₹30,000 crore (ADB and World Bank co-finance 50% of the central share as results-based loans), States ₹20,000 crore, Industry ₹10,000 crore, i.e. roughly 50:33:17. North-Eastern and hilly states use 74.7:8.3:17; UTs without legislature 83:0:17.

## Scale and cluster budget
1,000 government ITIs are upgraded as 200 Hub-and-Spoke clusters (200 hubs, 800 spokes, about four spokes per hub). A hub upgrade is capped at ₹81 crore and a spoke at ₹40 crore, so a typical cluster is about ₹241 crore over five years: Centre ₹112 crore, State ₹98 crore, Industry ₹31 crore, roughly ₹48 crore a year. Civil works may not exceed 20% of the budget. The central share funds capital expenditure. Industry must contribute at least 17% in cash; in-kind donations of equipment do not count toward the industry share.

## Component II
Five National Skill Training Institutes (Bhubaneswar, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kanpur, Ludhiana) become National Centres of Excellence for training of trainers. Central scheme management budget is ₹1,600 crore, including ₹600 crore for IT and digital infrastructure (the Learning Outcome Management System, LOMS) and ₹400 crore for PMU support (state PMU capped at ₹2 crore a year).

## Anchor Industry Partner (AIP) and SPV
A state issues an EOI and RFP; industry bidders submit a five-year Strategic Investment Plan (SIP). The State Steering Committee evaluates (technical 50%, financial 30%, industry contribution 20%) and the National Steering Committee approves. The winning AIP forms a cluster SPV as a Section 8 non-profit company with share capital ₹1 lakh: AIP 51%, Centre 24.5%, State 24.5%. Board of 11 directors: chairperson nominated by the AIP with government concurrence, four AIP nominees, two Centre, two State, one from existing ITI leadership, and the CEO. At least two directors must be women. The CEO is a full-time AIP nominee appointed by the board. The SPV signs a Shareholders' Agreement and a License Agreement (5 years, extendable). Some state RFPs (Rajasthan) require the AIP to run the cluster for 10 years with government funding only in years 1 to 5.

## Reserved matters
Decisions that need Central and State consent include any capital expenditure outside the SIP, operational expenditure outside the SIP, contracts above the SHA threshold, change of auditors, change in board or capital structure, asset disposal, borrowing, and any agreement between the SPV and the AIP itself. Intra-envelope budget reallocations can be approved by the board but must be communicated to the SSC and NSC within 15 days.

## Eligibility and status
AIPs can be companies, PSUs, industry associations or consortia, industry foundations, or industry-promoted academic institutions. States set turnover and employee thresholds (Rajasthan: ₹750 crore turnover, 1,000 employees; some states asked for ₹2,000 crore). As of October 2026, 14 SIPs worth ₹3,446 crore were approved across nine states and UTs, 850 ITIs identified (172 hubs, 678 spokes), and the nationwide rollout to all 200 clusters was approved on 7 July 2026. Approved AIPs include ArcelorMittal Nippon Steel India (Visakhapatnam, Surat), Jindal Naveen Avsar (Barbil, Kurukshetra), Apollo MedSkills, Neuland Foundation, Sri Siddharth Infratech, ZEN Technologies, H.G. Infra (Bhiwadi, first SHA signed), NSDC (Meerut), Belrise Industries and BVG Group.
